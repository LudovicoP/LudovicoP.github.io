End-of-module coursework for Applied Statistics, MSc in Statistics at Imperial College London. The work covers four problems spanning frequentist and Bayesian approaches: independence assumptions under clustering, modelling smoking intensity vs. binary status using Beta-Binomial conjugacy and Bayes factors, calibration noise models with posterior propriety analysis, and lung capacity regression using both GLMs and heavy-tailed Stan models.

PDF: <a href="/pdfs/AppliedStatistics-ICL.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener">View PDF</a>

## Methods

- Generalised linear models (GLM) with diagnostics
- Bayesian inference with Beta-Binomial conjugacy and Bayes factors
- Heavy-tailed regression via Stan (HMC)
- Posterior propriety analysis and prior sensitivity
